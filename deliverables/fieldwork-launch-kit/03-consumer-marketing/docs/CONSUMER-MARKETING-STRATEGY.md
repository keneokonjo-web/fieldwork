# Consumer marketing strategy

Version 0.1, 14 October 2026. For the founder. Covers Fieldwork for Individuals in the UK and Nigeria. Business (real estate) marketing is out of scope here.

**Status rule for everything below.** Every number is a hypothesis to test or a placeholder. Every platform fact marked *verify before use* must be checked on the platform before you rely on it. Nothing here is a result.

## 1. What exists today (the only things we may promote)

| Live | Planned (never promote as available) |
|---|---|
| One free guided exercise: brief, run, check, keep. About 15 minutes. No account. Entries stay on the device. Four starter tasks (weekly update, late-order reply, study notes, property listing) plus "your own task" | Guided projects (test price £14 / ₦7,500 one-off), membership (test price £9 / ₦4,500 a month), workflow library |
| Four Field Notes: the five-part brief; how to check an AI answer; five property tasks (and four to leave to people); what not to paste into an AI tool | Payments (not connected). Email tool (none connected yet) |
| An early-access email form on the site | Testimonials, case studies, customers, community: none exist |

Consequence: the job of consumer marketing for the first 90 days is **not selling**. It is (a) getting people to finish the free exercise, (b) building a small consented email list, and (c) learning what people would pay for. Paid products are tested, not sold.

## 2. Positioning

**Line:** Practise AI on real work. **Strategic idea:** Learn by doing. Put knowledge to work.

**One sentence for a stranger:** Fieldwork gives you a real task, a method and a way to check the result, free to start, with the free versions of tools you already use.

**Against alternatives (honest, no named competitors):**
- Versus tool tours and tutorials: we start from your task, not the tool's features.
- Versus prompt lists: we teach checking as well as asking.
- Versus influencer "AI hacks": we say what has not been tested and we publish limits.

**Proof we can honestly use today:** the exercise is live and needs no account; the method is documented in four Field Notes; the worked example in the exercise is clearly labelled as illustrative, not model output. **Proof we must not invent:** customer results, time saved, "thousands of learners", testimonials.

**Tone:** a colleague who tried it first. Plain words. British English (practise/practice). No hype words (revolutionise, unlock, supercharge). No fear of missing out. No income or productivity promises.

**Nigeria-specific constraints (design implications, ASSUMPTIONS to verify through interviews):** mobile-first; data cost matters, so prefer short, captioned video, text carousels and a site that works with free tools; payment must be in naira with local methods (provider still to be chosen); power and connectivity interruptions mean batch and schedule rather than post live. Never imply that UK content is "for everyone": produce a few NG-specific examples (naira, WhatsApp orders, local-sounding names) and ask Nigerian viewers what tasks matter.

**Do not stereotype.** Use examples, not assumptions about who a person is. Do not write in invented dialect.

## 4. Channels ranked

Ranking logic: (1) can a one-person, zero-budget team make good content weekly; (2) is our audience there; (3) does the format suit "show a task, check the result"; (4) can it lead to a measurable action (free exercise started). Reach numbers are not claimed; there are none yet.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Algorithm behaviour is not guaranteed |
| 2 | **WhatsApp** (personal sharing, Status, later a Channel; verify) | Central in Nigeria and widely used in UK; trusted referrals; good for interviews | Conversation and referral | 30 min | Do not spam. Mass messaging breaks the terms and the trust (verify rules). Consent first |

| 5 | **LinkedIn** | Work-related learners, UK professionals; good for text posts and carousels | Credibility and UK-A, NG-C | 2 posts a week | Format mix and reach rules change (verify). Do not post as the brand unless the company page is set up |
| 6 | **Instagram** (Reels, carousels, Stories) | Visual carousels of briefs and checks; reuse of the same video | Reuse and community | Reuse only, plus Stories | Duplicate-content handling (verify) |

**Not recommended yet:** paid ads (no evidence of conversion, no payment, no product to buy), X/Twitter, Facebook Groups at scale, Telegram, podcasts, influencer partnerships. Revisit after day 90 with data.

## 6. Hooks bank (pick, adapt, test)

The first three seconds decide whether anyone stays. Hooks should name a concrete task or a concrete mistake. Test results are unknown; treat as hypotheses.

**Do it**
1. Stop typing "write me an update". Do this instead.
2. A customer's order is late and your draft sounds defensive. Try this.
3. Turn one page of notes into a quiz for yourself.
4. Five parts of a brief. That is the whole trick.
5. You have 15 minutes. Here is a task you can finish.
6. Write the listing from the facts, not from the AI's imagination.
7. Messy notes in. A manager-ready update out. Checked.
8. The one line in a prompt that stops invented details.
9. How I brief an AI like a new colleague.
10. If your first answer was generic, your brief was thin.

**Check it**
11. The AI said it was done. The notes said it wasn't.
12. Confident does not mean correct. Here is proof on one paragraph.
13. Pick three details. Find them in your own notes. That is the check.
14. Ask the AI to catch its own made-up claims. Here is the prompt.
15. Two minutes of checking caught three errors. (Use only about the labelled illustrative example.)
16. Would you say this out loud? If not, it is not finished.
17. Read it aloud before you send it.
18. The sentence you cannot trace to your source is the one to cut.
19. A wrong amount sounds as sure as a right one.
20. Do not trust the summary. Trust the source.

**Tool honesty and safety**
21. Three things never to paste into an AI tool.
22. Green, amber or red? Before you paste it.
23. Use [CLIENT] and [ADDRESS]. Always.
24. You do not need a paid tool to start. Here is what the free version can do (check limits yourself).
25. I have not tested this tool yet. Here is what I did test.
26. Free tools change their limits. Check yours today.
27. What to do when the free version runs out mid-task.
28. No account, no card. Just try it.

**Field Notes**
29. Six checks that catch most AI mistakes.
30. Five property tasks worth practising, and four to leave to people.
31. The five-part brief on one page.
32. The traffic-light guide to what you can paste.

**Honest status**
33. Here is what Fieldwork is today and what it is not.
34. We have no testimonials yet. Here is why that is fine.
35. This is not for sale yet. Here is what we are testing first.
36. I got this wrong, and here is what changed. (Only if true.)
37. What would you pay for? Honestly, tell me.

**Community**
38. What is one weekly task you wish took half the time?
39. Which would you want first: plan, document or website?
40. Reply with a task and I will show how I would brief it.

## 7. CTA rules

1. **One CTA per post.** Never two.
2. **Match the CTA to the stage.** Cold viewers: try the free exercise. Warm: read the Field Note. Registered email: early access. Nothing says "buy".
3. **Say what happens.** "Free, about 15 minutes, no account" is a CTA. Do not say "limited".
4. **Never say a paid thing is available.** Use "planned" and "early access".
5. **No urgency or scarcity.** No countdowns, "last chance", "only X spots".
6. **No guarantee words.** No "will save you hours", no income promise.
7. **Every link has UTMs** (see `FUNNEL-AND-METRICS.md`) and points to a page that fulfils the promise (exercise, article, early-access form).
8. **Ask, don't push, for email.** The early-access form says "One email when something real is available".
9. **If a CTA cannot be honoured today (email tool missing), use the exercise CTA instead.**

Approved CTA lines: "Try the free exercise", "Read the Field Note", "Join early access (one email when something real is ready)", "Tell me what task to cover next".

## 8. What NOT to do

- No invented testimonials, learner counts, results, followers or "as seen in".
- No claim that guided projects, membership, a library or a community exist.
- No guarantees: income, grades, promotions, hours saved.
- No hype or fear language: "AI will take your job", "you're falling behind".
- No pretending to have tested a tool you have not. Say "not tested".
- No real customer or property data in demos. Use the fictional examples.
- No shaming of Nigerian or UK audiences, no stereotypes, no assumptions about tool access.
- No DM or WhatsApp mass messaging; no adding people to groups without consent; no buying followers or engagement.
- No scraping, no scheduling bots, no unverified growth hacks.
- No copyrighted music or other people's content without licence (see `VIDEO-PRODUCTION-KIT.md`).
- No advising on law, medicine, tax or property valuation. Fieldwork never suggests AI should value a property, give legal advice or approve a transaction.
- No academic dishonesty promotion: study content is about understanding, and users must follow their institution's rules.
- No sending email without consent and a configured tool.
- No tracking before consent; do not put emails or free text into analytics.

### Phase 2, days 31 to 60: Add depth and talk to people

Goal: email list, interviews, repeat topics.
- Welcome and post-exercise sequences go live (needs email tool and consent).
- 15 to 20 willingness-to-pay interviews (target, not guaranteed) using `CONSUMER-PRICING-ASSUMPTIONS.md`.
- Publish one new Field Note every 2 weeks.
- H5. At least some people name a task they would pay to be guided through: ≥10 of 20 interviewees name the same task cluster (placeholder).
- H6. Email subscribers open and click: open rate 35% to 55%, click rate 3% to 10% (placeholder; open rates are unreliable due to privacy features, verify).

### Phase 3, days 61 to 90: Test willingness to pay (fake door, honest)

Goal: decide whether to build guided project 1.
- Run a fake-door test on the early-access list: a page or email describing a planned guided project with price, a "Tell me when ready" button, and an explicit "not on sale" statement. See pricing doc for honest fake-door rules.
- H7. A meaningful share of recipients click through to the planned-project page: 3% to 10% of list (placeholder).
- H8. Of interviewees, willingness to pay is within the test price band (£14 / ₦7,500) at least for some (ASSUMPTION).
- Decision at day 90: build project one, change price, change audience, or stop.
