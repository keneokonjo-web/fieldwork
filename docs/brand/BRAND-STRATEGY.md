# Fieldwork brand strategy

Version 0.1, 14 October 2026. Written for the founder, future collaborators and contractors.

## 1. Purpose, mission, vision

**Purpose.** People learn tools best by using them on work that matters to them. Fieldwork exists to make that the normal way to learn AI.

**Mission.** Help people and teams get useful, checked results from AI tools on real tasks, through guided practice they can do in their own time.

**Long-term vision.** A trusted place where an individual can build a working habit with AI, and where a business, starting with real estate, can give its staff the same practice on its own workflows, then keep that knowledge in one place for new starters.

## 2. Positioning

For people who use or want to use AI for everyday work and study, and for small and mid-sized real estate businesses that want their staff to use it well, Fieldwork is the practical AI learning company that teaches through real tasks. Unlike courses that explain tools in the abstract, or consultancies that sell strategy, Fieldwork gives you a task, a method, a way to check the result and something finished at the end.

**Positioning statement in one line:** *Practise AI on real work.*

## 3. Audiences

### Individuals (UK and Nigeria)
Students, early-career professionals, independent workers, founders, creators, and the curious who do not consider themselves technical.

| Need | Frustration |
|---|---|
| Get a real task done faster or better | Tutorials show features, not outcomes |
| Know which tool suits which job | Too many tools, conflicting advice |
| Trust what comes out | Outputs sound confident and are sometimes wrong |
| Build a repeatable habit | One-off tricks that do not stick |
| Learn at a pace and price that fits | Courses that assume US prices, fast broadband and paid tools |

Market notes. UK: widespread access to free and paid tools, card payments, higher price tolerance for work-related learning. Nigeria: mobile-first, data costs matter, paid subscriptions priced in dollars are often out of reach or hard to pay for, local payment methods (card, bank transfer, USSD via a local gateway) matter. The free experience must work with free tools on a modest phone.

### Real estate businesses (UK and Nigeria)
Decision-makers: agency owners, directors, operations managers, heads of sales or lettings, marketing leads. Users: negotiators, lettings and sales staff, property managers, admin and marketing staff.

| Need | Frustration |
|---|---|
| Consistent, good-quality listings and communications | Every negotiator writes and works differently |
| Faster admin and follow-up | Time lost on repetitive writing and re-keying |
| Safe use of AI by staff | Staff already use tools without rules; owner worries about data and errors |
| Quicker onboarding of new starters | Knowledge lives in people's heads |

## 4. Brand promise

You will finish something real, and you will know how to check it.

## 5. Differentiation

1. **Task-first.** Every lesson starts from a job someone actually has.
2. **Checking is part of the method.** We teach how to evaluate output, not only how to produce it.
3. **Tool-agnostic and honest.** We say which tool does what, and we do not pretend to have tested something we have not.
4. **Works on modest means.** Free-tier friendly, light on data, priced per market.
5. **Independent.** No tool vendor sponsors the advice.
6. **Business: practice on your own workflows,** not a generic AI lecture.

## 6. Personality

Curious, practical, independent, creative, intelligent without being pretentious, confident without arrogance, approachable without being childish, contemporary without chasing every trend. Think: a good colleague who has already tried the thing and tells you plainly what worked.

Not: a school, a compliance vendor, a motivational influencer, a venture-funded AI template.

## 7. Voice

- Plain words, concrete nouns, short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Show the task before the theory.
- Say what a thing does and does not do.
- British English: *practise* (verb), *practice* (noun), *organise*, *colour*, *programme*, *whilst* avoided in favour of *while*.
- Numbers, tools and claims must be true and dated if they could change.
- Minimal em dashes. Prefer a full stop or a comma.

### Say / avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| task, workflow, brief, check, result, practise, draft, try | unlock, leverage, supercharge, revolutionise |
| "We have not tested this yet" | "Proven", "guaranteed", "game-changing" |
| "Early access" | "Limited time only", "Last chance" |
| "Your staff", "your listings" | "Empower your workforce" |
| "Check the output against the source" | "AI-powered insights" |
| "Planned", "not yet available" | Implying a product exists when it does not |

## 8. Messaging hierarchy

1. **Core idea:** Learn by doing. Put knowledge to work.
2. **Brand line (recommended tagline):** *Practise AI on real work.*
3. **Individuals promise:** Pick a real task, follow a guided method, check the result, keep a workflow you can reuse.
4. **Business promise:** Practical AI training for real estate teams, built around the work your staff already do.
5. **Proof (only what is true today):** The free experience is live and usable without an account. The method (brief, run, check, keep) is documented. Anything else is stated as planned.

### Value propositions

**Core.** Fieldwork teaches AI by having you do real work with it, then check and keep the result.

**Individuals.** Stop watching tutorials. Take a task you already have, follow a short guided method, and finish with something you can use and a workflow you can repeat.

**Business.** Your team practises AI on your own kinds of work (listings, enquiries, reports, procedures) with clear rules about what to use it for and what to leave to people. Delivered mostly as reusable material, with an optional live session.

### Reasons to believe (today)
- A working free guided experience anyone can try without an account.
- A published method and checking framework.
- Transparent product status: what exists, what is planned.
- No fabricated customers, results or testimonials. Evidence will be added as it is earned, with permission.

## 9. Tagline options

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Practise AI on real work.** | Clear, says what we do, British spelling, imperative. **Recommended.** |
| Learn by doing. Put knowledge to work. | Strong but long; keep as the strategic idea and as supporting copy. |
| Do the work. Learn the tool. | Punchy; slightly implies the tool is the point. |

| AI, by the job. | Compact; ambiguous. |

Final: **Practise AI on real work.** Supporting line: *Learn by doing. Put knowledge to work.*

Note on spelling: in British English "practise" is the verb. The tagline uses the verb. Nigerian English follows British convention here, so no adaptation is needed.

## 12. Name risk

"Fieldwork" is a common English word and an established term in UK market research ("fieldwork agencies"). A search on 14 October 2026 found several UK businesses using "Fieldwork" or close variants in that industry. This does not mean the name is unavailable for AI learning, but it raises trademark and search-visibility risks. See `docs/operations/DOMAIN-AND-TRADEMARK.md`. Take professional trademark advice before heavy investment.
