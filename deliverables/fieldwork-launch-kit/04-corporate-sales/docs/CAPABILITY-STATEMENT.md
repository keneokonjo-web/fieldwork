## 1. Who we are, plainly

Fieldwork is a new training company. We teach practical AI use through real tasks. Our first sector is real estate: estate agencies, letting agencies and property companies in the United Kingdom and Nigeria.

We are new. We have no client case studies, testimonials or published results, and we will not invent any. What exists today is:

- a free guided exercise anyone can try without an account;
- a published method called brief, run, check, keep;
- a workflow assessment on our website that gives a business a first, self-reported view of where practice would help;
- a way of delivering team training that we are now testing with first partner businesses.

If you want an established supplier with a long client list, we are not that yet. If you want a careful, independent provider who will say plainly what we know and do not know, and who will work on your own workflows, read on.

## 2. What we do

We help property teams get useful, checked results from AI tools on the work they already do: listings, enquiry replies, viewing feedback, research, internal reports, procedures and marketing posts.

We do not sell software, and we are not tied to any AI tool vendor. We recommend tools only for what we have actually tried, and we say when we have not tested something.

## 3. Our principle

**AI drafts. People decide.**

A tool can produce a first draft quickly. A person is responsible for what is true, what is sent and what is agreed. We never suggest AI should value a property, give legal advice, or approve an offer, tenancy or transaction. Training teaches staff where a tool helps, where it must not be used, and how to check what it produces.

## 4. Approach: brief, run, check, keep

| Step | What staff do | What we provide |
|---|---|---|
| **Brief** | Write a clear instruction for the tool, including what it must not do. | House brief templates, adapted to your tone and rules. |
| **Run** | Use an approved tool on a real (anonymised) example. | Guided practice tasks built from your own kinds of work. |
| **Check** | Compare every claim in the output with the source. Mark errors. | Checklists for each workflow, and a method for spotting invented detail. |
| **Keep** | Save what worked as a reusable workflow card. | Workflow cards your team owns and can edit after we leave. |

Most of the practice is reusable material that people work through in short sessions. A live or remote session is optional, not the core.

## 5. Workflows we can build practice around

| Workflow | AI can help with | People keep |
|---|---|---|
| Listing preparation and editing | First-draft descriptions from facts someone has confirmed | Every fact, from documents. Approval of each listing. |
| Enquiries and lead follow-up | Draft replies, viewing confirmations, follow-ups | Availability and price. Anything sensitive or negotiated. Sending. |
| Viewing feedback and updates to vendors or landlords | Summarising and structuring feedback notes | Judging what matters. Keeping negative feedback honest. |
| Research and organising information | Structuring notes and comparing documents you supply | Choosing sources. Verifying facts. |
| Internal documents and reports | Turning rough notes or exported figures into a readable summary | The numbers and the conclusions, checked against the source system. |
| Procedures and new-starter guides | Turning a written procedure into a checklist or quick guide | Accuracy. The procedure owner reviews every output. |
| Marketing content and social posts | Reshaping verified facts for different channels | Brand voice, claims, approval. |

We recommend starting with one or two. Most businesses should not try all seven at once.

## 6. Safeguards built into the training

1. **A written AI-use rule.** We help you draft a one-page rule for your staff: which tools are approved, what may and may not be pasted, who to ask. You own it and should have it reviewed by your own adviser.
2. **Checking against source, every time.** Each workflow includes a defined check step. Training uses real examples so staff see for themselves the kinds of error a tool can introduce, such as invented details or softened feedback.
3. **A not-for-AI list.** By default: valuations and pricing advice; legal, regulatory or safety statements (tenure, lease terms, planning, certificates, tenancy rights); language that could steer or discriminate, such as who a property is "suitable for" or comments on neighbours and neighbourhoods; approving offers, tenancies or transactions.
4. **Anonymised practice.** Staff practise on placeholders and anonymised examples, so personal data stays out of tools that are not approved for it.
5. **A named human owner.** We ask every client to name one person who owns the rules, the workflow cards and the checks.
