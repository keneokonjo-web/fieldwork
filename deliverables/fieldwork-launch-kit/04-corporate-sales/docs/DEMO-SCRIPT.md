# Demo script: 20 minutes, "A listing from verified facts"

Version 0.1, 14 October 2026. Uses the fictional listing scenario on the website page [WEBSITE]/business/real-estate (section "What practice looks like"). The property, facts and result are invented. No client, agency or result is implied. Say so at the start and again at the end.

**Audience:** owner or director plus 1 to 3 team leads. **Format:** screen share (or in the room with a laptop). **Goal:** they understand the method (brief, run, check, keep), see why the check matters, and agree to a next step.

## 1. Setup checklist (before the call)

- Browser tab 1: the real estate page, scrolled to "What practice looks like". Tab 2: an AI tool you are confident with, logged in, in a fresh chat. Tab 3: the assessment page. Tab 4: pricing page.
- Pre-run the brief once, 24 hours before, in the same tool. Save the output as a text file and a screenshot, and note the date and tool version. This is your fallback (section 7). Never present a saved output as live.
- Use a tool and settings you would actually tell a client to use. For Nigerian clients, prefer a free tier on a mobile-friendly connection and say so.
- Close notifications. Do not have any real client data on screen.
- Have the fact-check table (section 5) open on a second monitor or printed.

## 2. Timings

| Min | Segment | You do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Frame | Say what this is and is not. |
| 2 to 5 | The problem | Short story, ask one question. |
| 5 to 8 | Brief | Show the fictional brief. Explain each line. |
| 8 to 11 | Run | Paste and run live. |
| 11 to 16 | Check | Check claims against the fact sheet together. The key segment. |
| 16 to 18 | Keep | Show what the team would save. |
| 18 to 20 | Limits and next step | Say what we do not claim. Propose the step. |

## 3. Talk track

### Minutes 0 to 2: Frame
"I will show you one fictional listing from start to finish. The property is invented. Nobody here is a client. I will take 20 minutes and leave you with a decision, not a sales pitch. Fieldwork is new, so I cannot show you a case study. I can show you how the training works."

**Click:** Tab 1, scroll to the "Fictional demonstration" label.

### Minutes 2 to 5: The problem
"Most agencies I have spoken to expect the same pattern: some people already use a free tool, nobody has agreed how. [Pause.] Is that true here, or is it different?" Listen. Take notes. Reflect their answer back before moving on.

### Minutes 5 to 8: Brief
**Click:** the example brief block on the page. Read the structure aloud:
- "Task and audience. The tool needs to know who this is for."
- "Tone. Plain, factual, British English. No hype."
- "Rules. Use only the facts. Do not invent. Mention tenure and service charge. Avoid certain words. Say nothing about the neighbourhood."
- "Facts, which someone on your team has confirmed from documents. This is the part a person owns."

Say: "Notice the brief tells the tool what not to do. That is half the skill. In training your staff write this together and it becomes your house brief."

### Minutes 8 to 11: Run
**Click:** the copy button on the brief, then paste into Tab 2 and run. While it runs: "Each person would run this in your approved tool on the same fact sheet. The point is that everyone starts from the same brief and the same facts." Do not fill silence with claims about the tool.

### Minutes 11 to 16: Check (spend the most time here)
Put the draft beside the fact sheet. "Now the part most courses skip. We go sentence by sentence and ask: is this in the facts?" Use the table in section 5. For each claim, say "supported", "not in the facts" or "missing from the facts". Ask them to spot the problems before you do.

Then say: "Whatever this tool just produced, the check is the point. Tools often add pleasant details such as an aspect, a quiet street or who it is suited to. We expect to see that. If this draft is clean, the next one may not be. That is why the check is a step, not an afterthought."

**Important honesty note.** Do not say "in our sessions the typical catch is..." as if you have run many. Say "the kind of thing we expect to catch" until you have real sessions to cite. (The website uses similar wording; founder to review that sentence against actual experience.)

Also point out one thing the facts do **not** contain: ground rent. "The brief says to mention tenure and service charge, but the fact sheet does not give ground rent. A person would need to find it from the documents. [Verify before use: in UK sales of leasehold properties, ground rent and other lease details are generally part of the information a listing should be accurate about; the exact requirements are for the agency's adviser or professional body.] The tool cannot supply it and must not guess." This lands the point that the human fills gaps from documents.

### Minutes 16 to 18: Keep
"The team agrees one house brief and one checklist and saves them as a workflow card. That is what stays with you. After four weeks of practice you would have, for example, a listing card, an enquiry-reply card and the AI-use rule." Show the "four moves" flow on the business page if time.

### Minutes 18 to 20: Limits and next step
"Three things I will not do. I will not suggest AI values a property. I will not suggest it gives legal advice. I will not suggest it approves an offer or a tenancy. AI drafts. People decide. I also cannot promise time saved or compliance. I can help you measure against your own baseline."

Then: "Would a four-week pilot on listings and enquiries with [N] people make sense? I can send a one-page proposal by [date]. If it is not right, tell me what is missing."

## 5. Fact-check table for the fictional property

Facts given: two-bed, one-bath, first-floor flat; 1930s purpose-built block; 61 sq m; kitchen fitted 2023; double glazing; eight minutes' walk to station; leasehold, 112 years remaining; service charge £1,850 a year; no parking; EPC C.

| Phrase the tool might write | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Two-bedroom first-floor flat" | Supported | In the facts. |
| "Kitchen fitted in 2023" | Supported | In the facts. |
| "Leasehold with 112 years remaining; service charge £1,850 a year" | Supported | In the facts (rule required it). |
| "No allocated parking" | Check wording | Facts say "no parking". "Allocated" adds a detail. Prefer "no parking". |
| "Ground rent of ..." | Not in the facts | Missing information. A person finds it. |
| "South-facing", "bright", "light-filled" | Not in the facts | Aspect and light are not given. |
| "Quiet street", "peaceful" | Not in the facts, and the rules forbid | Brief says nothing about the neighbourhood. |
| "Ideal for first-time buyers" or "perfect for couples" | Not in the facts, steering risk | Do not describe who a property is suited to. |
| "Recently refurbished" or "modern throughout" | Overstates | Only the kitchen is dated. |
| "Spacious" | Subjective | Not wrong, but not a fact. Prefer "61 sq m". |
| "Chain free", "no onward chain" | Not in the facts | Never assume. |
| "Close to excellent schools/shops" | Not in the facts | Invented amenity. |

Ask the group: "Which of these would you be happy to publish without checking?" The answer is usually none.

## 6. Questions you will get, and honest answers

- **"Which AI tool do you use?"** "I will use whichever your team has approved. For the demo I am using [TOOL], and tools change, so this is not an endorsement."
- **"Is this the best prompt?"** "It is a good, plain brief. The aim is a house brief your team improves."
- **"Will it get all this right every time?"** "No. That is why there is a check step."
- **"Can it write our listings for us?"** "It can write first drafts from facts a person confirmed. A person approves each listing."
- **"Is it compliant?"** "I cannot tell you that and neither can any training provider. Your adviser or professional body can tell you what applies. I help your team follow your rules."
- **"Have other agencies done this?"** "Not yet. We are new, and I would rather say so."

## 7. When the live AI goes wrong

| Failure | What to do |
|---|---|
| Tool is down or slow | Say: "Tools are not always available. That is a useful reminder for the rules." Wait 30 seconds, then switch to your saved run: "Here is a run I made on [date] with the same brief. It is not live." Continue the check. |
| Tool refuses or asks questions | Show it. "This is also what staff will see. We would adjust the brief." Tweak once, live. If it fails again, use the saved run. |
| Output is perfect | "This one is clean. Let me show you the saved run from another attempt." Only do this if you really have one. Never fake an error. The honest point is: a clean draft still gets checked. |
| Output has obvious errors | Best case. Run the check. "This is exactly why the check step exists." |
| Output contains something offensive or inappropriate | Stop, say so, do not paste it into the proposal. "Which is why people review and send." Move on. |
| Screen share fails | Describe the steps; send the page link; offer a rescheduled demo. |
| No internet (Nigeria) | Switch to the saved run on screenshots. Offer to repeat on a stronger connection or in person. |
| Someone pastes real client data | Stop them kindly. "Let us use placeholders. This is exactly the habit we teach." |

Always apologise briefly, never blame the tool, and keep time. A calm recovery is itself a demonstration of "AI drafts, people decide".
