# Outreach kit

Version 0.1, 14 October 2026. For a first-time seller approaching estate agencies, letting agencies and property companies in the UK and Nigeria. All messages are drafts for the founder to personalise. **Never claim clients, results or partnerships. Say Fieldwork is new.** Regulatory notes at the end are general, marked verify, and not legal advice.

## 1. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Hypothesis to test, not fact.**

| Attribute | Best fit | Acceptable | Avoid for now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Independent estate or lettings agency; small property group; property management firm | Developer sales office; serviced-accommodation operator | Portals, large corporates, software vendors (partners instead) |
| Size | 8 to 60 staff, 1 to 5 branches | 5 to 8 or 60 to 100 | Sole traders (too small for a pilot); over 100 (procurement too slow) |
| Signs of fit | High listing volume; several negotiators; staff using AI informally; recent hire(s); owner still involved in operations | Owner open to trying new things | Active reorganisation; owner disengaged; heavy dependence on a franchisor's mandated tools |
| Geography | UK: any region. Nigeria: Lagos and Abuja first [verify market priorities] | Port Harcourt, Ibadan, others | Markets where you cannot serve the language, time zone or payment context |
| Trigger events | New branch, new software, new starters, regulator or redress-scheme reminders, an error in a listing | Seasonal peak | None obvious |

## 2. Three buyer personas (hypotheses, built from the brand strategy; validate in discovery)

### Persona 1: "The owner-director"
- **Who:** Founder or managing director of an independent agency, 10 to 40 staff.
- **Cares about:** reputation, consistency, risk, margin, not wasting time on fads.
- **Fears:** a wrong listing, a data mistake, staff using tools they cannot see.
- **Says:** "I don't know what my people are putting into these tools."
- **We say:** "A one-page rule and a method your team follows, with people still deciding."
- **Proof they need:** a fictional worked example, a small fixed-fee pilot, an easy exit.

### Persona 2: "The operations manager"
- **Who:** Operations, lettings or compliance manager.
- **Cares about:** procedures followed, quality consistency, onboarding speed, workload.
- **Fears:** another project with no time; inconsistent output from the team.
- **Says:** "Everyone writes differently and new starters take months."
- **We say:** "Workflow cards and checklists that you own, built on your procedures."
- **Proof they need:** clear scope, hours required from staff, plan for measurement.

### Persona 3: "The head of marketing or sales"
- **Who:** Marketing lead, head of sales or lettings.
- **Cares about:** listing quality, speed to market, brand voice, lead follow-up.
- **Fears:** hype, off-brand output, steering or misleading language.
- **Says:** "We need good descriptions faster, without sounding generic."
- **We say:** "House briefs that encode your voice and rules, and a check against the facts."
- **Proof they need:** the listing demo and the not-for-AI list.

## 3. Building a target list

**Method (about 3 hours for 50 accounts):**
1. **Define a patch.** One UK region or city and Lagos or Abuja. Start with 50 accounts per market.
2. **Sources (public, lawful):**
   - Portal agent directories and the agencies' own websites (UK and Nigeria portals; verify the list before use).
   - Professional body member directories where public: UK (e.g. Propertymark, The Property Ombudsman or redress-scheme members lists, NRLA) and Nigeria (e.g. NIESV, ESVARBON, REDAN) [all verify: availability, terms of use for directory data].
   - Google Maps and LinkedIn Company pages for size and staff.
   - Local business associations and chambers of commerce.
3. **Qualify on public signals:** staff count (LinkedIn), branches, listing volume, evidence of recent hiring, quality and consistency of listings (without criticising).
4. **Find the right person:** owner, director or operations manager. One contact per account to start.
5. **Record in the CRM** (CRM-TEMPLATE.csv): source, date, lawful-basis note (section 9), score.
6. **Prioritise:** tier A (fits ICP, trigger present), B (fits), C (watch). Contact A first.
7. **Cap volume:** 10 new contacts a week, personalised. Quality over volume.

Do not scrape personal emails, buy unverified lists without checking their provenance, or use data in ways inconsistent with the terms of the source.

## 6. Three-step follow-up cadence

| Step | When | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Email (or LinkedIn note if connected) | Emails 1 to 5 |
| 2 | Day 4 | LinkedIn or WhatsApp (Nigeria) | "Sent you a note on [topic]. Is it useful to see a 20-minute fictional example?" Add one new piece of value, e.g. the assessment link |
| 3 | Day 9 | Email | Short bump: "One thing I didn't mention: [fact-check table / free exercise]. Worth a call?" |
| Close | Day 16 | Email 6 | Breakup. Then stop for 90 days unless they engage |

Rules: maximum three touches plus the breakup; stop on any "no" or unsubscribe; log every touch; vary the angle, not just "bumping this".

## 11. Compliance notes (general; mark verify; not legal advice)

### United Kingdom
- **PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations)** governs electronic marketing. As generally understood: unsolicited marketing emails to **corporate subscribers** (limited companies, LLPs, public bodies) can be sent without prior consent, but you must identify yourself, give a valid contact address and offer a clear way to opt out; marketing to **individual subscribers**, which can include sole traders and some partnerships, generally needs consent or a soft opt-in. Role-based business addresses (info@) at companies are treated differently from named individuals at sole-trader practices. **Verify with the ICO's current guidance or a solicitor before sending.**
- **UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018** still apply to personal data (names, work emails). You need a lawful basis (legitimate interests is commonly used for B2B prospecting, with a documented balancing test) and a privacy notice that tells people who you are and how to object. **Verify.**
- **Telephone:** check the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) and Corporate TPS before cold calls. **Verify.**
- **LinkedIn:** follow LinkedIn's terms; do not automate mass messages.
- Always include your company name, address and a simple "reply STOP / unsubscribe" line in cold emails. Keep a suppression list.

### Nigeria
- The **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023** applies to personal data processing, including prospect lists, and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission has issued regulations [verify the current instruments]. Consider lawful basis, transparency and individuals' right to object. **Verify.**
- Direct marketing by SMS, call or WhatsApp may be subject to telecoms rules (NCC) and consumer protection rules, including a Do-Not-Disturb mechanism for SMS [verify: current rules and how they apply to WhatsApp and email]. **Verify.**
- Business culture often favours warm introductions and calls. Use referrals and association events where possible.

### Everywhere
- Do not state or imply clients, partnerships or results that do not exist.
- Record the source and date of every contact; delete on request; honour opt-outs immediately.
- Keep personal data minimal: name, role, company, work email, source.
