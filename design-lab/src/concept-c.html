Concept C

The Press

Fieldwork as an independent publication. Colossal serif type, saturated spreads, numbered issues of practical work.

01Rationale

The best way to make practical learning feel worth reading is to publish it like it matters. Field Notes already reads like a series. This direction makes the whole brand a magazine.

Oversized Instrument Serif headlines with italic emphasis, full-bleed colour spreads in plum, coral, marigold and lilac, big folio numerals for exercises and issues, pull quotes, and arch-shaped panels that frame worksheets like plates in a book. Warm, bold, confident, with a clear editorial voice. Each exercise is a numbered feature, and the business side becomes a printed report with the same typography.

The learning grammar

Coral: your move. Instructions and prompts.

Lilac: the example. Plates and worked samples.

Marigold: the highlight. The line worth underlining.

10Strengths, weaknesses, risks

Strengths

  • Most editorial: makes Field Notes and exercises feel like a publication worth reading.
  • Distinctive, warm, confident. The lilac and plum palette avoids the beige and green default.
  • Elegant wordmark that costs nothing to produce and scales.
  • Folio numerals and arch plates make a strong, repeatable system.

Weaknesses

  • Single-weight display serif limits hierarchy tools.
  • Less tactile and less obviously about practice and making than A.
  • Colourful spreads need care in dense learning interfaces.

Risks

  • Magazine or luxury feel could drift towards lifestyle brand.
  • Arch motif is currently common in interiors branding.
  • Not yet checked against the real references.
