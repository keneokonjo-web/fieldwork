01Idea and what it borrows

A quiet, photographic, gallery register. Slow, spacious, and confident enough to leave things out.

This direction is closest to how Compound presents design-led property and editorial work: thin tracked labels, light serif headlines, generous margins, and objects shown with their own captions. Because Fieldwork has no photography yet, the "photographs" are plates: framed prints of the generated field, hung on dark or chalk walls with museum-style labels (Plate, Specimen, Filed).

It is the most elegant, and the best fit if the brand wants to feel like quiet-luxury property publishing. It is also the one that most depends on real photography to succeed: without photographs of real places and working surfaces, it can feel empty.

Contrast

05Wordmark and symbol

Fieldwork
Fieldwork

Tracked capitals in Cormorant with a survey benchmark mark: a ring with a centre point and four ticks, the symbol surveyors use for a fixed reference. It says "a known point to measure from", which is what a checked workflow is. Quiet and exact. It drops its ticks at 40px and fills at 18px for favicons.

10Strengths, weaknesses, risks

Strengths

  • The most elegant and the closest to real estate editorial and developer branding.
  • Quietly credible for business buyers; slow and uncluttered for learners.
  • Plates of terrain are original, cheap to produce and framed like art.

Weaknesses

  • Delicate type and thin lines need generous space and careful contrast.
  • Least colourful: weaker at-a-glance on social and video.
  • Learning grammar is subtle (label colours, not colour blocks).

Risks

  • Depends on real photography to avoid feeling empty, and we have none yet.
  • Could drift toward luxury lifestyle, which is wrong for a practical brand.
  • Cormorant's fine strokes fail at small sizes and on low-quality screens.
